# Qwen3.8-Flash-Next on M5 Max: what the ecosystem has that we do not

Research date 2026-09-12. Baseline: oMLX 0.7.0.dev2 (mlx 0.32.2, mlx-lm 0.31.3, mlx-vlm 0.6.3), `Qwen3.8-Flash-Next-oQ4e-mtp`, MacBook Pro M5 Max 128 GB, macOS 26.5. Measured: cold 65k prefill 1438 tok/s, decode 61.5 tok/s with MTP.

Version facts checked today: mlx 0.32.2 is the newest release (PyPI, 2026-08-25). mlx-lm 0.31.3 is newest. mlx-vlm 0.7.0 shipped 2026-09-07, so we are one minor version behind there. oMLX 0.7.0.dev2 (2026-09-11) is newest, so we are current on the server.

## Ranked candidates

### 1. Upgrade mlx-vlm 0.6.3 to 0.7.0

Source: https://github.com/Blaizzy/mlx-vlm/releases/tag/v0.7.0 (2026-09-07, requires mlx >= 0.32.2, which we have).

Changelog lines that hit our exact path: "Optimize Qwen4 n-gram shard gathers", "Optimize exact Qwen verifier QMV unrolling", "Optimize Qwen4 QSA cache reuse and batched parity", "adapt MTP draft depth", "add external PLE storage", "Fix native quantized KV in Qwen MTP verification", "chunk prefill for prompts shorter than prefill_step_size". Four of those touch decode, which is where our gap is.

Expected: decode +10 to 25 percent, prefill single digits. Needs: oMLX pinned to mlx-vlm 0.6.3, so either a constraint bump or vendoring the qwen4_exp module. The n-gram shard gather optimisation may collide with our packed layout patch, so diff before merging.

### 2. Turn on oMLX's own INT8-activation prefill

Source: https://github.com/jundot/omlx/releases/tag/v0.7.0.dev2. Quoted: "32K prefill 615.2 tok/s (INT8 OFF) vs 826.7 tok/s (+34.4%)" on M5 Max, covering Qwen3.5, Qwen3.6 and Qwen3.8. Experimental, disabled by default.

We already have the code and have not enabled it. It is a strictly larger win than our own unmerged int8 MoE gather kernel (+5 percent per chunk). Needs: a config flag, no upgrade. Verify it actually engages for `qwen4_exp` MoE experts and not only dense projections.

Underlying method and eligibility rules are public: https://github.com/JetBrains/mlx-vlm/tree/feature/int8-prefill/research. W8A8, per-token dynamic symmetric activations, per-channel weights derived on the fly from the 4-bit weights, eligibility `N%128==0, K%32==0, min dim >= 1024, N <= 32768`. Measured 1.37x at 10k and 1.41x at 20k on M5 Max with Qwen3.6-27B-4bit, nothing below a few thousand tokens. Our 512-expert top-10 shapes need checking against `N%128==0`.

### 3. MLX PR #4481, matrix kernels for global-scale gather_qqmm

Source: https://github.com/ml-explore/mlx/pull/4481, "Use matrix kernels for global-scale gather_qqmm", opened 2026-09-08, still open. Reported M5 Max prompt throughput 1257.0 to 3105.3 tok/s, generation unchanged at ~81.8. M3 Ultra 1627.3 to 2673.2.

This is the single biggest prefill number anywhere in the MLX tree right now and it is the MoE gather path. Needs: a main-branch mlx build and a global-scale (oQ-style) checkpoint. Unverified against our checkpoint's quant layout. Related smaller open PRs worth taking in the same build: #4483 "Load global scales in qmm_t kernels" (2026-09-09), #4487 "Improve metal memory usage for SDPA D512" (2026-09-10), and #3912 "Fix fp quantized matmul corruption when the quantized dim is not a multiple of 32" (merged 2026-09-11).

### 4. Rebuild the MTP verify path the way MTPLX did

Source: https://mtplx.com/how-it-works/ and https://mtplx.com/benchmarks/. Their fork carries `verify_qmv` small-M qmv, a "4-simdgroup" variant with `unroll_count(4) for verify shapes M=3..6`, `sdpa_nax_flash` flash-decoding verify attention, a `linear-gdn-from-conv-tape` fused GDN verify kernel, and a draft-only 4/3-bit LM head. Acceptance measured at depth 4 on Qwen 3.6 27B: D1 97.62, D2 95.24, D3 88.10, D4 75.61 percent.

Their published M5 Max Flash-Next figure is 61 tok/s plain decode and 63 to 76 with the MTP head, and 68.4 tok/s at 16k on MTPLX 2.11 (measurements 2026-08-29 to 09-04). Our 61.5 with MTP sits at their plain-decode level, which says our verify pass is eating the speculative gain rather than our base decode being slow. That makes the small-M verify kernels the highest-value decode work.

Also already merged upstream and free: https://github.com/ml-explore/mlx/pull/3791 "Raise qmv batch limit for large matrices on M5-class GPUs" (merged 2026-08-08), lifting the `get_qmv_batch_limit` threshold from 10 to 13 on gen-17 for D>4096, O>4096, worth 18 percent on a 70B whole-model forward at M=12. It is in 0.32.2, so we have it, but it only pays off if we run verify batches above M=10, i.e. MTP depth beyond 3 or batched verify.

### 5. Speculation policy, not kernels

Source: https://github.com/ddalcu/mlx-serve/releases/tag/v26.9.2 (2026-09-09). On M4 Max, Flash Next goes "83 to 93 tokens per second, with +50% at short prompts and +30% at 64k and 128k". Three named changes: speculative decoding "costs less per step, reaches full speed on the first request instead of the fifteenth", the drafter "picks its draft from a small shortlist instead of reading the whole vocabulary", and speculation switches itself off when it stops paying at long context.

M4 Max and M5 Max have comparable memory bandwidth, so 83 to 93 tok/s against our 61.5 is a real 35 to 50 percent gap with no exotic hardware involved. The shortlisted-vocabulary drafter is cheap to copy. Earlier v26.9.1 also reports long-prompt prefill 32k 589 to 699, 128k 395 to 654, 256k 267 to 551 tok/s.

### 6. MTP depth tuning, with a warning

vLLM's own recipe for this model (https://recipes.vllm.ai/Qwen/Qwen3.8-Flash-Next) recommends `num_speculative_tokens: 3` but reports that on 4x H100 it "made throughput worse at every concurrency tested" at roughly 36 percent acceptance. Depth is hardware-specific and must be swept locally. Given MTPLX's 88 percent D3 acceptance on a smaller Qwen, our depth 3 is probably right and depth 4 is worth one sweep, especially combined with #3791's raised qmv limit.

### 7. Do not chase native int4 tensor ops

Evidence points the other way. Apple's own writeup (https://machinelearning.apple.com/research/exploring-llms-mlx-m5) requires macOS 26.2+ and describes TensorOps and Metal Performance Primitives, with prefill up to ~4x over M4 and decode only 1.19 to 1.27x because decode is bandwidth bound. BaseRT (arXiv:2607.19438v1, 2026-07-21, M5 Pro 48 GB) routes prompt GEMM, MoE expert GEMM and flash attention through `matmul2d` cooperative tensors using 4-bit and 8-bit packed weights with int8 accumulation, and reports prefill up to 6.4x versus llama.cpp and 3.9x versus MLX, decode 0.98 to 1.33x versus MLX. Notably its MoE margin is only 2.2x at pp2048 versus 6.3x dense. Nowhere does it use a native int4b matmul. Corroborating detail from https://github.com/pawel-mazurkiewicz/ComfyUI-AppleSilicon-FP8/issues/17: the int8/int4 shaders use "only `bfloat`, `int8`, and packed `int4b` types plus `mpp::tensor_ops::matmul2d`, all available in MSL 4.0", i.e. int4b is a storage format fed to an int8 tensor op, not a separate int4 math path. Secondary sources claim M5 has no cooperative-input matmul intrinsics for int4b; treat that as unverified, but the direction is clear. Our own roofline (int8 129.8 vs int8 x int4 120.0 TFLOPS) says the same thing: int4 operands are slightly slower than int8 x int8, so the int8-activation route is the one to push.

### 8. Nothing new for Gated DeltaNet on Metal

No MLX PR for a fused GDN or chunked delta-rule Metal kernel exists. Upstream GDN work in 2026 is all Triton in fla-org/flash-linear-attention (GDN-2 added May 2026, PR #950 for PGDN/KDA on 2026-06-17) and CUDA/Blackwell. MTPLX's `linear-gdn-from-conv-tape` verify kernel is the only Apple-side GDN kernel described publicly, and it is closed. Writing one is a build, not an upgrade.

## Decode headroom arithmetic

6B active parameters at oQ4e (roughly 4.5 effective bits with scales) is about 3.4 GB read per token, plus a fixed GDN state, plus about 1.4 KB of n-gram rows (16 lookups at ~90 bytes), plus a small QSA selected-K/V read since the model picks top-512 blocks, roughly 2048 tokens, out of the full cache. Against our measured 549 GB/s copy ceiling that is a hard bound near 160 tok/s. A scattered 512-expert top-10 gather realistically sustains 60 to 75 percent of copy, so call the practical ceiling 100 to 120 tok/s. We are at 61.5, about 38 percent of the copy bound and roughly 55 percent of the practical gather bound. mlx-serve's 83 to 93 tok/s at similar bandwidth confirms the room is there and that it comes from speculative efficiency rather than raw weight streaming.

## Already at the frontier

Our 1438 tok/s cold 65k prefill is ahead of every published Flash-Next prefill number found: oMLX 0.7.0.dev1 reports 1113.3 tok/s at 16K on M3 Ultra, MTPLX 2.11 reports 810 tok/s on a 131k prompt, oMLX dev2's INT8 path reaches 826.7 at 32K on M5 Max, mlx-serve v26.9.1 reaches 699 at 32k, and llama.cpp on a 128 GB M5 Max measures 966 tok/s at zero KV depth falling to 80 at 262k (https://heretik.io/qwen38-flash-next-262k-macbook/, 2026-08-28). Contexts differ, so this is indicative rather than a clean comparison, but we are not behind on prefill.

The packed contiguous n-gram layout is genuinely at the frontier and slightly ahead of upstream. The equivalent idea is still an open PR in llama.cpp: https://github.com/ggml-org/llama.cpp/pull/28136, `--lazy-mode on-direct`, replacing mmap demand faults with parallel `pread()` because a 4 KB page is faulted to serve a ~90 byte row. It reports 2.0 to 3.1x cold prefill on GB10 and up to +272 percent on RTX 5090, but on Metal and M5 Max testers found warm performance "wash to marginally slower", with the real benefit being flat memory headroom rather than throughput. Our 3x amplification figure matches their 4 KB per 90 bytes analysis exactly. Worth knowing: their worker count matters enormously, 1 worker was only ~1.15x over serialised faults while the default 32-worker pool added ~6.6x, so if our packed reader is single-threaded that is a free win.

Our roofline numbers are consistent with public figures, so no correction needed there: bf16 65.7 and int8 129.8 TFLOPS reproduces the widely cited ~2x int8 advantage on M5 neural accelerators.

MLX 0.32.2's release note "Add a fused full-attention path for head_dim 256 on NAX devices" applies directly to this model, whose 12 attention layers use head_dim 256 with 2 KV heads. We are on 0.32.2 so we have the kernel, but confirm oMLX's exact-QSA path (added in oMLX 0.6.4, 2026-08-29, which reports +33.5 percent prompt processing at 32K on M3 Ultra) routes through it rather than around it.

## Caveats

Release dates for oMLX 0.7.0.dev1 and dev2 (2026-09-10 and 09-11) are one and two days old, so their numbers have no independent confirmation. MTPLX and mlx-serve figures are vendor-published, single-stream, fans at maximum, and not reproducible from source in the MTPLX case. PR #4481's 2.47x prefill gain is from the PR author's own benchmark on an unnamed MoE checkpoint. No M5 Ultra benchmarks for this model exist yet in any source found.
