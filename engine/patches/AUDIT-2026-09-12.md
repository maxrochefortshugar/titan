# Audit: Qwen3.8-Flash-Next (qwen4_exp) on oMLX 0.7.0.dev2, M5 Max 128 GB

2026-09-12. Read-only. Production on 8083 untouched apart from `GET /health` (healthy, 78.0 GB
resident, 100 GiB ceiling). New numbers come from synthetic microbenchmarks under 1 GB with
`kdev/bin/python`; the rest is code reading or existing results. Paths are relative to
`/Applications/oMLX.app/Contents/Resources`, and `LANG` =
`omlx/patches/mlx_vlm_qwen4_exp_compat/vendor/mlx_vlm/models/qwen4_exp/language.py`.

## Three corrections to the existing record

**1. The read-only bandwidth ceiling on this machine is 718 GB/s, not 549.** A 734 MB reduce
sustains 718 GB/s; read+write copy is 557. Every decode roofline in `kernels/REPORT.md` and
`kernel-notes.md` used the copy figure. From the safetensors headers, a decode forward reads
**4.20 GB** of text-path weights (routed experts 1.24 GB at top-10 of 512, GDN 1.25, lm_head
0.63, QSA ~0.40, hyper-connections 0.34, shared expert 0.23, router 0.12), so the roofline is
**5.9 ms per forward = 171 tok/s**, not 133.

**2. `profile-flashnext/` loaded a model missing eight post-load patches, and it turns out none
of them matter here.** `load_omlx.py` replays only the pre-load patches, the gate/up fusion and
sdpa256; `VLMBatchedEngine.start` also applies patches at `omlx/engine/vlm.py:2135, 2146, 2165,
2182-2187, 2198, 2212, 2376, 2391`. I checked each against this model: `qwen35_moe_weighted_sum`
is gated to `top_k in (6, 8)` (`qwen35_moe_weighted_sum.py:58`) and Flash-Next is top-10;
`qwen35_moe_router`'s fused top-k caps at 8 rows (`qwen35_moe_router.py:33, 139`), so prefill
never routes; `qwen35_gdn_prework`'s qwen4 arm is 1-row decode only (`:507-537`);
`qwen35_verify_sdpa_split` and `qwen35_verify_qmm` are verify-only; `qwen35_ragged_decode` is
left-padded batch decode only; `qwen35_q4_mlp` targets qwen3_5's dense `Attention`/`MLP`. The one
that does rebind a Flash-Next path, `qwen35_gdn_chunked.py:99-100`, I benchmarked: its
`gated_delta_blocked_seq` at T=2048 with 48 value heads measures **3.60 ms** against the profile's
stock 3.43, since its advertised 2x is at 16k. So the profile is a valid production proxy at a
2048-token chunk, and the corollary matters: **profile fix 6 is not already done.**

**3. Prefill does not pay for `lm_head`.** `omlx/models/vlm.py:183-185` declares
`supports_skip_lm_head` for `qwen4_exp` and `:464-470` turns it into `skip_logits=True`, so the
48-95 ms per chunk in the profile's table 8 is never spent in production.

## A. Where the time goes

### Per 2048-token prefill chunk

Measured = `profile-flashnext/r_ablate2048.json` ablation differencing (paired baselines, 12 s
cooldown, min of 3) and `kernels/ple-fix/REPORT.md` §1-2. Production applies the deployed bf16
norm and packed-PLE patches.

| item | measured | production | achieved / ceiling |
|---|---|---|---|

| - of which the two gather matmuls | 413 ms | 413 ms | roofline 3.06 ms/layer against 8.61 actual |
| - of which unsort + score-weighted sum | 67 ms | 67 ms | pure data movement, no kernel available (see D2) |
| GDN x36 | 366 ms | 366 ms | in_proj 49.7 TF/s (76%); **scan 123 ms at 5%** |

| QSA x12, empty cache (indexer bails) | 84 ms | 84 ms | dense SDPA |

| PLE table lookup, warm ids / cold ids | 18 / 822 ms | **6 / 456 ms** | 158x read amplification |

Production measures **1438 tok/s** on a cold 65k prefill (`prod/bench-prod-20260912.json`) =
1413 ms per chunk averaged over 32 chunks. The ~400 ms gap per chunk is context growth, and it
is budgeted nowhere. `ple-fix/REPORT.md` §3 measured the slope directly: over a 32k prefill,
fully patched, per-chunk time runs 992 -> 1472 ms, and "the patched run is flatter, so the
advantage narrows to about 1.10x by chunk 16". Single-stream prefill takes the gathered sparse
path (`LANG:1272-1302`, eligible once `cache.offset + T > 2048`), so this is gathered-QSA and KV
traffic, not the dense fallback. Under concurrency it is worse: batched or padded prefill falls
to `LANG:1226-1253`, which materialises a `[batch, seq_len, key_len]` boolean mask per QSA layer
(134 MB per layer at 65k), and the scheduler additionally drops the chunk to 512 tokens when
decode is contended (`scheduler.py:1542-1550, 5177-5197`), which the profile measured as 22%
worse per token.

**Top three prefill costs: (1) attention over the growing cache, ~400 ms/chunk averaged at 65k
and unprofiled; (2) the routed-expert gathers, 413 ms at 35-37% of the bf16 tensor ceiling;
(3) the GDN layers, 366 ms, of which the recurrent scan is 123 ms at 5% of ceiling.**

### Per decode step

There is no per-component decode profile; the table is **estimated** from measured per-kernel
costs and byte counts, and the total is checked against the measured step. Measured anchors:
61.5 tok/s with MTP on, 41 tok/s with MTP off = **24.4 ms per single-row forward**. The MTP
cycle is one draft chain of 3 plus **one** verify forward of M = k+1 = 4 rows
(`mlx_lm_mtp/batch_generator.py:2933-2953`, depth 3 from `omlx/utils/model_loading.py:721`,
adaptive 1..3), with a single host sync for acceptance (`:2981-2990`). Backing out from the two
measured rates gives ~1.8-2.0 accepted tokens per ~30-33 ms cycle.

| item | est. ms per 1-row forward | basis |
|---|---|---|
| GDN x36 (4 in_projs, conv, 2 norms, scan, out_proj, ~10 launches each) | ~9.0 | dense qmv at ~330 GB/s plus launch floor |
| MoE routed experts x48 | ~4.8 | **measured** 0.061 ms gate_up + ~0.04 down per layer |
| lm_head | ~0.9 | **measured** 0.930 ms, M=1, 8-bit |
| QSA x12 plus KV reads (1.6 GB at 65k) | ~3.0 | byte count at 718 GB/s |
| PLE n-gram lookup (host + SSD) | ~2.3 | derived: the rows patch moved decode 47.9 -> 61.7 tok/s = -4.7 ms at a 3x page cut |
| hyper-connections x96 | ~2.0 | small-tensor launches, 1-16 row fused path |
| 3 draft passes (MTP layer + lm_head each) | ~4.0 | 3 x (0.93 lm_head + ~0.4) |
| **total** | **~26** | vs 24.4 ms measured for the MTP-off forward |

Measured, not modelled: the expert gather runs at **300 GB/s at T=1 (42% of 718), 490 at T=4,
549 at T=8**, so the verify forward at 4 rows costs 0.151 ms per layer where four separate
single-row steps would cost 0.244. `lm_head` at M=1 hits 726 GB/s, at the ceiling, but M=8 falls
off a cliff (4.23 ms against 1.51 at M=4 and 1.70 at M=16): M=8 lands in `affine_qmv_wide` while
M=16 reaches `affine_qmm_t_nax`, so the fix at those widths is padding rows to 16, not a new
kernel. oMLX never presents 5-12 rows to the head today, so this is latent rather than live.
`COMMON.md`'s "lm_head M=1 0.454 ms" is a 4-bit number; the checkpoint ships 8-bit, where I
measure 0.930 ms.

**Top three decode costs: (1) the 36 GDN layers, ~35% of the forward, at ~46% of read bandwidth
across ~360 launches; (2) the expert gathers at 42% of bandwidth purely for lack of rows;
(3) the vocabulary head, paid four times per cycle because every draft samples.** The PLE
lookup is still ~9% of a forward and is host/SSD work, not GPU.

## C. What actually runs on NAX

The device reports `applegpu_g17s`, and `metal::is_nax_available()` (`libmlx.dylib` 0xdae1bc)
returns true for gen >= 17. Dispatch was read out of `QuantizedMatmul::eval_gpu` (0xe0607c) and
`GatherQMM::eval_gpu` (0xe069c0): on this GPU the row threshold is **13** for both q_proj
(K=2560, N=6144) and lm_head (K=2560, N=248320), 33 only when K and N are both <= 2048.

| matmul | M | kernel actually launched | on NAX? | int4 operands? |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| dense projections, prefill | 2048 | `affine_qmm_t_nax`, BM=64 | yes, 57.0 TF/s = **87% of the bf16 ceiling** | no, bf16 after per-tile staging |
| dense projections | 13-32 | `affine_qmm_t_splitk`: `cap = max(1, 512/(n_tiles*m_tiles))` gives split=2 at N=6144, so it never reaches `qmm` | **no** | no |
| dense projections | 1 / 2-12 | `affine_qmv_fast` (K=2560 is %512) / `affine_qmv_wide` nv 2..5 | no | no |
| **expert gather, prefill** | rows=20480 | sorted fast path: `M==1 && B>=16 && B/E>=4` holds (B=20480, E=512), so `gather_qmm_rhs` -> `affine_gather_qmm_rhs_nax`, and `rows_per_expert = 40 < 64` selects **BM=32** (0xe042b8) | yes, 23-27 TF/s = **35-40%** | no, same bf16 threadgroup staging |
| expert gather, decode/verify | 10-40 rows | B=10 or 40 fails `B>=16 && B/E>=4` at E=512, so `gather_qmv` | never | n/a, memory bound |
| lm_head | 1 / 2-12 / >=13 | `affine_qmv_fast` / `affine_qmv_wide` / `affine_qmm_t_nax` (cap collapses to 1 at n_tiles=7760) | only >= 13 rows | no |
| MTP verify | 4 | `qwen35_verify_qmm.py:418-431` routes M=3..6 at N >= 16384, so only `lm_head`; split-K bf16 | no | no |

**Yes, the prefill expert gather runs on the tensor units, with bf16 operands, and the reason it
only reaches 35-40% is visible in the tiling.** Two things, in order of size. First,
`quantized_nax.h:638, 646, 673` stage dequantised weights into `threadgroup T Ws[]`
(`:1244, 1503`) before `tile_matmad_nax` (`:1053-1059`, with a `volatile int compiler_barrier`
at `:1042` in the inner loop), and `steel/gemm/nax.h:401-424` instantiates
`mpp::tensor_ops::matmul2d` with `AType = BType = T`, i.e. bf16 only. The only int4 operand type
anywhere in the tree is CUDA's (`backend/cuda/quantized/qmm/qmm_utils.h:47`); `mlx.metallib`
contains no `int4b`/`uint4b_t` at all, though `MTLTensor*` symbols are present. Second, BM=32
against 40 rows per expert means two row tiles per expert and **every expert weight streamed
twice**; that is precisely what `moe-int8/`'s ragged TMT=48 tiling fixed for 1.48x. Note the
interaction: a 4096-token chunk would get 80 rows/expert and BM=64, but `m5_gather_qmm.py`
segments anything above 32768 rows into ~20480-row slices, which puts rows_per_expert back to 40
and BM back to 32.

So K%32, group size and sorted indices are **not** what blocks int4: `moe-int8/` built the kernel
for this exact ragged shape. What blocks deployment is 11.3 GB of transposed scale/qsum tables,
which does not fit under the 100 GiB guard beside a 78 GB model and a KV cache. What blocks it
inside mlx is that 0.32.2's NAX support is transposed-only, BK=64-only and bf16-staged-only:
`affine_qmm_n_nax` is compiled (216 instantiations) but unreachable behind the `transpose != 0`
gate, `affine_gather_qmm_n_nax` has zero instantiations, and there is no `fp_qmm_n_nax`.

## E. Verdict

Prefill is close to the hardware where the hardware is the binding constraint, and the remaining
inefficiency is concentrated in two places that are not kernel-tuning problems. The dense
projections sit at 76-87% of the 65.7 TF/s bf16 tensor ceiling and will not move without int4
operands that mlx does not offer; the expert gathers sit at 35-37% and a kernel that fixes them
exists but costs 11.3 GB the machine does not have; the recurrent scan sits at 5% because it is
a sequential recurrence. On a 2048-token chunk from an empty cache the body is about 1000 ms
against a mixed roofline near 600, so 60-65% of practical peak, and the deployed patches took
the cheap bit-safe part of that. Production's 1438 tok/s over a 65k prompt is well under the
2048 tok/s the same body delivers at chunk 1, and the whole difference is attention over the
growing cache, which nobody has profiled. Decode is much further out: 4.20 GB per forward is
5.9 ms at this machine's real 718 GB/s read ceiling, so 171 tok/s for one token per step, and
production delivers 61.5 tok/s from roughly two accepted tokens per 30 ms cycle, about 25-35%
of roofline. That gap is not bandwidth. It is occupancy and launch count at one row (the expert
gather achieves 300 GB/s at T=1 and 549 at T=8), plus ~2.3 ms per forward of host-side n-gram
lookup and four sequential vocabulary passes per cycle. Every remaining decode lever is
therefore about getting more rows into each kernel - MTP acceptance, concurrency, the fused
decode kernels - or removing the SSD table, and the last of those needs the 256 GB Ultra.
